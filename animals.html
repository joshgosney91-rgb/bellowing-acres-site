80+ Residents

Our Residents

Every animal here has a story. Every story started with a rescue. Meet the remarkable individuals that make Bellowing Acres home.

Alligators

American Alligators

Over 80 rescued gators call Bellowing Acres home — every one saved from euthanasia after being labeled a nuisance.

🐊
Alligator

Bubba

American Alligator · Alligator mississippiensis · 9 ft

Bubba was removed from a residential pond in Palatka after repeatedly approaching homeowners. At 9 feet long, relocation was never an option. He arrived at Bellowing Acres in 2022 and has become one of our most personable residents.

Sponsor Bubba
🐊
Alligator

Duchess

American Alligator · Alligator mississippiensis · 7.5 ft

Duchess was found injured on a highway in Putnam County — likely hit by a vehicle during nesting season. After treatment for her injuries, she couldn't be released. She now rules the eastern pond with quiet authority.

Sponsor Duchess
🐊
Alligator

Swamp King

American Alligator · Alligator mississippiensis · 11 ft

Our largest resident, Swamp King was deemed a public safety threat near a marina in St. Johns County. Trappers called us when they learned what fate awaited him. He is breathtaking — and thriving.

Sponsor Swamp King
🐊
Alligator

Cypress

American Alligator · Alligator mississippiensis · 5 ft

Cypress was surrendered as a juvenile illegally kept as a pet. He arrived malnourished and stressed. Under Chris and Gabby's care, he's grown into a healthy, habituated gator who loves the sanctuary's natural habitat.

Sponsor Cypress
🐊
Alligator

Magnolia

American Alligator · Alligator mississippiensis · 6.5 ft

Magnolia was relocated three times before wildlife officials determined she couldn't be moved again. Each time, she returned to human settlements. Bellowing Acres became her permanent home in 2023.

Sponsor Magnolia
🐊
Alligator

Colonel

American Alligator · Alligator mississippiensis · 8 ft

Colonel is one of our oldest residents, estimated to be over 40 years old. He was removed from a golf course pond where he'd spent most of his life. Calm and stoic, he's a favorite with tour guests.

Sponsor Colonel
Crocodiles

Crocodiles

Among the rarest and most awe-inspiring animals at the sanctuary — each one with a remarkable origin story.

🦎
Nile Crocodile

Akila

Nile Crocodile · Crocodylus niloticus · 10 ft

Akila was donated to Bellowing Acres by Gatorland Orlando — one of the most storied reptile attractions in the world. Her arrival marked a pivotal moment for the sanctuary and cemented its reputation for caring for rare species.

Sponsor Akila
🦎
American Crocodile

Ghost

American Crocodile · Crocodylus acutus · 7 ft

Ghost arrived after being confiscated by Florida Fish and Wildlife from an unlicensed facility. American crocodiles are a federally threatened species — his placement at Bellowing Acres was coordinated with wildlife officials to ensure proper care.

Sponsor Ghost
Exotic Animals

Exotic Animals

Reptiles, birds, and small mammals — each surrendered or rescued, now thriving in a safe and loving environment.

🐢
Tortoise

Titan

Sulcata Tortoise · Centrochelys sulcata · 120 lbs

Titan was surrendered by a family who bought him as a "small tortoise" not realizing sulcatas grow to over 150 lbs. Now thriving on the sanctuary's grassy acres, Titan is a fan favorite for tour guests of all ages.

Sponsor Titan
🦜
Macaw

Scarlet

Blue & Gold Macaw · Ara ararauna · Age ~15 yrs

Scarlet was surrendered by an elderly owner who could no longer provide care. Macaws live up to 60 years — Scarlet needed a permanent home. She's feisty, vocal, and the loudest personality at the sanctuary by a wide margin.

Sponsor Scarlet
🦎
Tegu

Goliath

Argentine B&W Tegu · Salvator merianae · 4.5 ft

Goliath was confiscated in Alachua County — part of Florida's ongoing invasive species problem. Tegus cannot be returned to the wild. He's one of the most handleable animals at Bellowing Acres and a hit on educational tours.

Sponsor Goliath
🐍
Python

Shadow

Ball Python · Python regius · 5 ft

Shadow was surrendered after her owner moved to an apartment that didn't allow pets. Ball pythons live up to 30 years in captivity. She's healthy, calm, and lives in a beautifully designed enclosure at the sanctuary.

Sponsor Shadow
